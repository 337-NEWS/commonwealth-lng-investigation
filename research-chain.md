# Research Chain: Commonwealth LNG, Cameron Parish and ITEP

Updated: October 5, 2026

## Scope
This working record tracks Commonwealth LNG's existing Louisiana Industrial Tax Exemption Program contracts, its 2026 pursuit of enhanced 93% exemption treatment in Cameron Parish, the parish response, governor's-office involvement, and related campaign-finance records.

A disclosed campaign contribution does not establish influence, coordination, quid pro quo, or causation.

## Verified chronology

### July 15, 2019 — Existing ITEP contract
Louisiana executed Commonwealth LNG ITEP Contract **#20180509-ITE**, associated with the original roughly $3.9 billion Cameron Parish facility. February 2026 Board of Commerce & Industry materials also identify Commonwealth Contract **#20220302-ITE**.

Commonwealth therefore already participated in ITEP before the 2026 dispute.

### March 20, 2025 — Enhanced mega-project tier
Gov. Jeff Landry issued **Executive Order JML 25-033**. The mega-project framework permits enhanced ITEP treatment for qualifying projects above $500 million, including exemption percentages in the **93%-100% range**, through the ITEP/Board of Commerce & Industry process. Implementing rules are in Louisiana Administrative Code Title 13.

The 93% pathway existed before Commonwealth's May 2026 final investment decision.

### December 31, 2025 — Campaign contribution
**Commonwealth LNG LLC contributed $12,000 to Jeff Landry's campaign.**

- Report: **LA-132570**, Landry 2025 annual filing.
- Verification: **one $12,000 contribution**. Byte-identical duplicate portal search rows from the same non-amended report are display duplicates and must not be double-counted.

This establishes a disclosed contribution only.

### February 25, 2026 — BCI contract amendment
Commonwealth returned to the Louisiana Board of Commerce & Industry for an amendment involving its existing ITEP arrangement. Public materials describe project-timing/phased-asset changes, including an extension through December 31, 2031.

This was a **contract amendment**, not a February 2026 amendment of Louisiana's ITEP rules.

Open item: preserve the complete agenda/minutes and identify precisely which Commonwealth contract or contracts the action amended.

### May 2026 — Final investment decision
Commonwealth/Caturus announced final investment decision for the Cameron Parish LNG facility, then described at approximately **$13 billion**.

Contemporaneous Caturus and Louisiana Economic Development releases contain company-leadership quotations but no located statement that Commonwealth would terminate or abandon its existing ITEP contracts.

#### Quote-provenance caution
The Center Square reported/paraphrased that Commonwealth would not participate in the local ITEP. 337.NEWS has **not located a direct quotation from Commonwealth Chairman Paul Varello Dell saying this**. The Caturus and LED FID releases contain Dell quotations but no equivalent ITEP statement. A syndicated copy later located on HoumaToday omitted the ITEP sentence.

Accordingly:
- Do not present the statement as a direct Dell quote.
- If used, attribute the characterization to The Center Square.
- Do not state as established fact that Commonwealth "reversed" a direct public promise without stronger primary evidence.

The documentary record does show that Commonwealth already held ITEP contracts and later sought enhanced treatment under that same program.

### Earlier in 2026 — 93% request
Cameron Parish DA Tom Barrett has described Commonwealth as seeking to move from **80% exempt / 20% taxable** treatment to **93% exempt / 7% taxable**.

No exact date for the initial 93% request has been located. Barrett's public formulation is "earlier this year."

Barrett says the request first went to parish officials and the local ITEP committee, which was not receptive. No public-meeting record of that initial request has been located.

### After local opposition — Governor's office involvement
According to Barrett, Commonwealth then pursued its position with the governor's office. Barrett says representatives from the governor's office asked him to discuss a compromise.

No public source reviewed identifies those representatives by name.

### September 14, 2026 — Industrial-development-board route
By the September 14 Cameron Parish Police Jury meeting, the governor's office was involved and an industrial-development-board route was being discussed.

The contemplated structure could place qualifying industrial assets with a public entity and lease them to Commonwealth, replacing conventional ad valorem taxation with negotiated payments in lieu of taxes (PILOTs) and potentially other contractual terms.

The IDB mechanism was not completed; the resolution remained under development.

### September–October 2026 — PILOT projections
Barrett projected potential Commonwealth payments of approximately:

- **$150 million during years 1-10**
- **$1 billion during the following decade**

These are projections, not an executed agreement, guaranteed payment schedule, assessor valuation, or taxing-body fiscal estimate.

### Early October 2026 — Application still pending
As of the latest reviewed reporting, Commonwealth had **not filed the amended application** that would formally trigger the local ITEP committee's 45-day response period. The proposed IDB resolution also remained in draft.

## Fiscal-data limitation
No publicly located Cameron Parish assessor, school board, sheriff, or other taxing-body calculation quantifies Commonwealth's actual revenue difference between 80% and 93% exemption.

### Comparator: Cheniere
Cheniere's separate Cameron Parish 93% matter provides scale only. Public reporting calculated approximately:

- $29.8 million conventional annual property taxes
- $10.5 million estimated annual taxes under 93% treatment
- approximately **$19.3 million per year difference**
- approximately **$138 million projected tax benefit**

These are **not Commonwealth figures**.

Campaign-finance research separately identified **Cheniere LNG O&M Services LLC — $12,000 to Jeff Landry on November 18, 2025**. This is likewise a disclosed contribution, not evidence of influence.

## Editorial standard
Report dates, filings, amounts, government actions and attributable statements without asserting that campaign contributions caused, purchased or influenced official action absent evidence establishing such a relationship.

Where primary records conflict with, qualify, or fail to substantiate secondary reporting, state that explicitly.
